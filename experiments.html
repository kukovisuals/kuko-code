What it can measure: how often each line ran on the GPU, where on screen the GPU time went, and the text the compiler produced. Close the city tab first, so it isn't drawing on the same GPU.

Your code, line by line measured

Source: a counting copy of your shader adds one counter per { } block and writes the counts instead of a color, and readPixels reads them back. Your if and for statements take the same path, so the counts are exact. Lines in one block share its numbers. runs / frame: times the block ran over all 1,024,000 pixels of one frame. max / pixel: the most for one pixel. pixels: share of pixels where it ran at all. lanes waiting modeled: a SIMD group of 32 pixels goes through a block as many times as its busiest pixel, so this is the share of those lane-slots whose pixel had already finished. 32 is a community figure (metal-benchmarks). The 8 x 4 layout is ours. Click a line to see where its block ran.

The compiler

Two compilers sit between your GLSL and the GPU. Chrome's ANGLE rewrites it for Metal, then Apple's compiler turns that into GPU code. A page can read the first one's output and nothing after it.
